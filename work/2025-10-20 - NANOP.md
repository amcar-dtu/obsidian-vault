### Enhancing the PL of hBN Triplet Defect through Energy Transfer for Improved Sensing
*<u>Ms. Eveline Mayner</u>$^1$ , Prof. Aleksandra Radenovic $^1$* 
*1 LBEN EPFL*

Interesting to see that the last author is the same leader in this publication! 
Glushkov, E. _et al._ Engineering Optically Active Defects in Hexagonal Boron Nitride Using Focused Ion Beam and Water. _ACS Nano_ **16**, 3695–3703 (2022) [https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsnano.1c07086](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsnano.1c07086).

Seems like they kinda went away from $V_B^-$, at least judging from the Scholar of A. Radenovic, but looking at the poster here seems like they want to give it another try, but with low effort. 

They have very efficient $V_B^-$ generation (neutron irradiate bulk crystal and then exfoliate, like V. Jacques) and very efficient PL excitation (at 488 nm, close to the PLE max at 478 nm), but they have poor control (they don't check) over the flakes thicknesses, so they have very poor PL from $V_B^-$ (the hBN is too thin and they use a very cheap, fiber-coupled spectrometer, so even for 1 s int. time they detect <0.05 kcnts/s in the $V_B^-$ region).

However $-$ and this is the spirit of the work $-$ by creating an heterostructure (HS) with $hBN$/$PbI_2$/$hBN$:$V_B^-$/$hBN$ (here $PbI_2$ is called lead iodide). The point is that the interface $PbI_2$/$hBN$:$V_B^-$ creates a quasi-type I band alignment:

![[Disegno 1.png|300]]

The idea is that in $PbI_2$ there are two kind of excitons (one well know, the primary, around 500 nm, and another, less bright, not studied, which they believe is a self-trapped exciton, around 700 nm), and excitons in $PbI_2$ couple through the band alignment to $V_B^-$ in hBN, bringing electron-hole pairs in the $V_B^-$ state, thus enhancing a lot the luminescence.
As a matter of fact, in the HS area the luminescence around 800 nm is enhanced, and resembles the one of $V_B^-$, whereas the exciton from the $PbI_2$ is quenched. They report a 16x increase in PL compared to the non-HS area, and a 5x increase in the ODMR contrast.

Open points:
- the PL emission from the $V_B^-$ in the HS area is blueshifted (more around 780 nm) compared to the usual one
- the mechanism according to which this stuff works is not fully clear to me, weil the band alignment and this exciton coupling should take place only in the depth where hBN and $PbI_2$ are very close. If it were a mechanism similar to the one reported in graphene/$hBN$:$V_B^-$/graphene HSs (see Fraunié, J. _et al._ "Charge State Tuning of Spin Defects in Hexagonal Boron Nitride". _Nano Letters_ **25**, 5836–5842 (2025) [https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c00654](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c00654)) then why do we see an improvement of PL/ODMR instead of a quenching, as reported by Fraunié, J. _et al._?
- the $V_B^-$ PL improvement is also visible in areas where there is no hBN, and the girl was not sure why that, and since they don't have good hyperspectral maps it's not clear so far
- the choice of $PbI_2$ was just from trial and error, looking among vdW materials with compatible bandgap
- the girl meant that one way this mechanism works so well is because the $PbI_2$ is a good absorber, so it makes sense to look at the picture as: hBN is a bad absorber (that's why the poor quantum efficiency), and by charge migration $PbI_2$ can transfer e-h pairs to the hBN and therefore enhance the PL emission. If instead you consider the $V_B^-$ as a poorly emitting system (owing to strong ISC), then the picture makes less sense, because the emitters should be "busy" in their dark state.
  But it is also true that bringing more carriers to the emitters by carrier diffusion can enhance the chances of PL emission (as a more efficient pumping), and this mechanism can be more effective than the one from Fraunié *et al.* because it is known that through a graphene gate electrons are easier to inject than holes, so in such pictures one can understand that in the latter case the recombination between carriers can be hindered.
